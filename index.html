Metadata as Storytelling in Voca

Voca is an audiovisual archive of poetry readings at the Poetry Center Library at the University of Arizona.

Each reading is broken into tracks.

Consider this Spring 2025 reading from American poet and educator Terrance Hayes, which can be found here.

Poetry readings are more than just poetry. They often begin with an introduction to the reading.

Readers also can give commentary about their work to enrich how the audience experiences a poem.

But at the heart of the reading are the poems.

Some were published long ago. Some were published recently. Some were unpublished at the time, or uncollected. To explore the uncollected, take a look at Understanding the Uncollected.

The poems and themes from one reading are put into conversation with other readings in Voca through subject tags. To explore tags in depth, take a look at Tumultuous Tags.

Sometimes moderators or the live audience get to ask questions after a reading. But what about those watching online? See Making Metapoetry for some ideas on how to actively participate in these poetic conversations.

The composition of tracks varies between readings. Readings can contain translations, prose, and lectures, too. Some tracks resist classification by blending these categorical boundaries.
