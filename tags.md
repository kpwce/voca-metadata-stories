# Tumultuous Tags

The readings in Voca are each associated with one or more tags. Some of the readings have tags associated with individual poems, while others only have tags associated with the reading as a whole. This disparity is a result of inconsistent grant funding for the Poetry Center’s digitization projects.

The tags themselves include thousands of words. We scraped these from the site and put them into Google Sheets to enable our data exploration. Looking at the thousands of words in our spreadsheet, we felt both overwhelmed and excited; overwhelmed in anticipation of trying to coax meaning from so much data and excited by the variations of language in the list. Voca is an archive of poetry, and many of the tags reflect that subject matter with terms like ‘[inside/outside worlds](https://voca.arizona.edu/items-with-tag/5965/5965),’ ‘[ultratranslation](https://voca.arizona.edu/items-with-tag/4835/4835),’ ‘[non-existence](https://voca.arizona.edu/items-with-tag/6409/6409)’ and many others that seem to reflect a taggers’ response to the reading more than any sort of search optimization.

## Why explore digital tags?

The data on institutional digital archives like Voca may surprise users with its evidence of human creation. Despite a clean, alphabetized digital interface, the tags contain language that resists standardization and that reflects individual experiences with the recordings. For example, the tags ‘surreal’ and ‘surrealism’ both appear. (maybe find different example). An automated tagger would likely have collapsed these into one term. The existence of both shows that without a standard language, separate taggers will make slightly different associations with similar content.

This lack of standardization is an issue that is frequently present in digital collections and digital archives today (citation?). However, it is uncertain whether manual tagging practices like these will remain the norm whether new tools such as LLMs will result in a more standardized digital landscape.

Standardized digital tagging systems offer many benefits to digital archives. They create data that is more user-friendly, more search-engine friendly, and likely in smaller amounts. The purpose of this essay is not to argue for the use of purposefully messy data. Instead, we are curious to discover the narratives that are contained in this kind of jointly created metadata.
